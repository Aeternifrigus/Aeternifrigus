Why do humans believe in something? Do they have to? Or, do they need to? Let it be religious beliefs, ethical beliefs, beliefs pertaining to survival or beliefs essential to give the life lived a purpose. They all have their own purposes.

Looking at the life of a human being: when it comes to this world, completely oblivious of self and the space around, it tries to imitate the space and beings around. First, learns of physical pain, then comfort, then joy and sorrow, in order, depending on circumstances.

When it learns to communicate, the first and foremost thing it learns about itself is that it's an individual entity and becomes aware of its own isolated existence. Independent of circumstances, its interactions with both living and non-living. No matter what, it exists by the testament of self. What a paradox. (A piece for some other day)

Gains insight about life, how ephemeral it is, and how it feels. Learns about feelings and emotions. It's different from unintelligent creatures, but still the same. After looking at itself, it looks around. Or in some, looks around and then looks at itself. When looking around, it learns of relations, bonds, society and the world, the sandbox it exists in.

Given the idea that its existence relies on it being in the sandbox. This world defines whether itself is existing or not, acknowledging the interactions it performs as part of a history that no one can change. Then, how should one interact? Analyse? Understand? Sense? Feel? This calm and slow yet chaotic and fast world… Natural and artificial. Humans and nature. And self. This amalgamation is what they call life. What to do? How to? Why to?

It navigates the world with the values and teachings imposed on it, at first. Agnostic of anything and devoid of experiences and the capability of processing them. They build the AXIOM OF THE LOGIC TREE and ROOT BELIEF OF WRONG AND RIGHT, AND HOW IT IS SUPPOSED TO FEEL in varying circumstances. A bit of imitation and a bit of direct teaching.

Evolving through time, trying to adapt, it adjusts and learns. Scraps some axioms, holds dear to some. Maybe for not succumbing to an existential crisis or to not have a moral breakdown, or simply just for utility and consequences. If Logic is an artificial abstraction, then emotion, too, is not all organic, and you can teach yourself what to feel when and then train to make it natural for you.

Sometimes it lets go of that armour of logic to come out in its emotional vulnerabilities because it was simply way too suffocating inside, and sometimes it just coops up inside out of paranoia. Sometimes it's a meticulously designed execution of logic inspired by an emotional agenda. Failing to realise what it feels strongly about, what it doesn't feel anything about; what it focuses on whilst analysing and what it overlooks or doesn't assign importance to, is all influenced by its subconscious biases. Biases that are architected by the experiences and realisations from them.

Then again, all those anecdotes are formulated and inspired and made up biases biased by BELEIFS. Certainly, beliefs are not stagnant and ever evolving, yet, somehow, it leads to something that's predictable from stage 0 to stage 1. Not something outlandish or unpredictable. So is it really something in its control or a mere possibility out of finitely infinite set? (So much for the word "destiny")

Self is both internal and external, yet what it sees and processes are all internal, where it's heavily influenced by beliefs. Is it possible to break it apart? Build again from scratch? Yes, it is. Reincarnation, they call it, in some faiths. Cause even if one makes a conscious attempt to break it, you will only break aspects of it that you could through experiences. Not the ones you couldn't cause you wouldn't even think of it. IT BLINDS YOU. Emotionally too.

Are there people without anything that they believe in? NO. Even an animal believes in death. WHY DOES IT MATTER? Logically, it may be nonsensical to succumb to it and emotionally, it may feel manipulative. But that feels like we are under the impression that the world outside has something that's not this. A blind man only knows of light cause of its external interactions; in the same way, the world is that external that made us cognisant of that LIGHT.

In the same paradoxical manner, we get toyed by this whole architecture(world, self, living, non-living, existence) that encapsulates us and throws fulcrums at us. I want to ask, "IS THIS WORLD LOGICAL?" As I once told my friend Mota, " THIS WORLD IS NOT. WE ARE NOT SUPPOSED TO MAKE SENSE OUT OF IT. WE ARE JUST TO EXPLORE, GIVEN WHATEVER OUR ROMANTICS ARE." So…Questioning the handicraft of our beliefs in our "self" and struggles to break free from it, relying on the possibility that this nonsensical world showed us, is probably LOGICALLY "ABSURD" and EMOTIONALLY VINDICTIVE.
