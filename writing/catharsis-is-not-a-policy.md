*Critic from an idealistic stance*

*The Embarkation for Cythera (1717) by Jean-Antoine Watteau*

Two movements, one failure mode. Both start from a real grievance, and both reach for a tool that feels like power. Neither ties that tool to a number that would show the grievance was fixed. This is the critic's case. The opposing view is at the end.

## 1. When is protest legitimate in a democracy?

Four gates:

1.1. Evidenced harm. A specific, documented injury, not a mood.

1.2. Remedy gap. Courts, legislatures, and elections are too slow or have demonstrably failed. The case is strongest when the harm is irreversible. and time-bound, such as a result finalised or a poll held on a contested roll.

1.3. Lawful, non-coercive form. A protest's authority comes from the legal order it appeals to. Peaceful assembly keeps it. Threatening the state's functioning to force compliance forfeits it.

1.4. Demand quality. The demand must pass three checks.

A protest compresses time inside a channel. It does not replace the channel.

## 2. The three checks

- **Variable:** does the demand act on the causal variable?
- **Stress:** does it still work if the actors are adversarial or incompetent?
- **Falsifiability:** can you state in advance what outcome counts as success?

## 3. CJP protest 1: NEET and exam bodies

Legitimacy: valid. CJP's own charter counts over 90 leaks in a decade across the NTA, SSC, and state bodies. Admissions run on a fixed schedule, so the harm is time-bound, and an anti-leak law already existed without stopping it. Gates 1 and 2 pass. cockroachjantaparty

The demands included Pradhan's resignation, Wangchuk's release, and ₹1 crore compensation for families, alongside the structural charter. Wikipedia

What happened after. For over three weeks, the agitation revolved around one demand: the minister's resignation. The charter came on 15 July, ten days before the resignation and the call-off. Reporting lists the core demands behind the withdrawal as the resignation, Wangchuk's release and the compensation. Tribune Exclusive: Beyond Pradhan's resignation, Cockroach Janta Party unveils charter for overhaul of exam system - The Tribune +3

Reforms did follow. The government cancelled NEET-UG, ordered a CBI probe, announced a computer-based format and began restructuring the NTA, and the resignation came as one part of a larger overhaul. Around 47 officials were reportedly removed, with tighter outsourcing rules. A Nilekani-led task force is reviewing NTA exams and the Supreme Court is monitoring reforms through the year. If the structural measures came with or before the resignation, the resignation was neither necessary nor sufficient (Demand centering the protest), and crediting it is post hoc reasoning. An educator quoted by the Tribune said replacing the NTA alone would not help unless exam infrastructure itself were strengthened, which is the Variable check from outside. Can Dharmendra Pradhan's exit and Centre's new education reforms restore faith in India's exam system? +3

I found no CJP scorecard comparing enacted measures with its five demands. The movement declared victory on an input before any output existed. That is the central flaw. The movement did not die: it widened its demands to the Prime Minister's and Home Minister's resignations. No logic here for the movement's widened demands.

## 4. CJP protest 2: SIR

Legitimacy: valid. Polls proceed while rolls are revised, and a deleted voter cannot be restored after the election. Litigation is slower than the calendar. CJP cites reports that two Election Commissioners objected at least 14 times in 10 months and that over 13 crore names were struck from draft rolls, which I treat as its claims. cockroachjantaparty

Critique of the failing demands:

**Immediate criminal proceedings against the CEC.** This demand is a category error. Criminal liability requires a finding of fact and an identified offence, and an inquiry exists precisely to produce both. Demanding prosecution before the inquiry inverts the sequence and presupposes the conclusion the inquiry is meant to reach. It also destroys the movement's own premise: a protest in defence of due process cannot coherently demand punishment without process. On the Stress check, the demand fails against any adversary with a defence, since an unsupported prosecution would collapse and rehabilitate its target. On Falsifiability, no evidence could count against it. The question "who gave the orders" assumes a superior conspirator whose existence is the thing to be established.

**Freezing all upcoming elections.** The remedy is disproportionate to the disease. A dispute over roll accuracy affects a bounded set of constituencies and voters, whereas a national freeze suspends the franchise of every voter, including the many whose entries are uncontested. The measure also introduces a worse attack surface: whoever holds the power to freeze elections holds a lever over the democratic calendar, and the demand does not specify who would hold it or under what test it would end. A targeted, constituency-level stay on contested deletions achieves the legitimate aim at a fraction of the cost, and the pending petitions already seek relief of this kind. Finally, no end condition is stated: the freeze lasts until an inquiry reports, and the inquiry has no deadline.

**Rollback to the January 2025 list.** This substitutes one error for another. A rollback can err in two ways: wrongful deletion (a valid voter removed) and wrongful retention (an ineligible entry kept). Reverting to the older list reduces the first error type by guaranteeing the second, restoring duplicates, deceased voters, and migrated entries along with any wrongly deleted names. The statistically proper instrument is a sampled audit that estimates both error rates and then selectively corrects deletions. As the matter is sub judice, there is a further difficulty: the demand asks the street to order the reversal of a process the Court has examined and, as reported, upheld, while the narrower question of specific deletions awaits its ruling. Pressing for a blanket rollback bypasses the very forum capable of a proportionate remedy.

**The duplicative demands.** The inquiry and the file-custody requests are sound in design but redundant in forum, since the same relief sits before the Supreme Court in petitions with stronger standing and evidentiary routes. Their value on the street is as an expression of urgency and not as an independent demand. A movement serious about outcomes would have supported the pending petitions with data, for instance by publishing a verified sample of wrongful deletions, which would have given the bench evidence rather than rhetoric.

The pattern from protest 1 repeats. Demands that target a mechanism (audit, custody, appointment design) pass. Demands that target a person or a blunt freeze fail.

## 5. Sharjeel Imam [A parallel, not guilt by association]

Imam's name appeared in both protests, which is a point of both resemblance and irony. A Goa police affidavit cites a placard bearing "Umar Khalid, Sharjeel Imam, Bhima Koregaon" at a 25 July NEET-protest dharna at Azad Maidan. In the SIR phase, AISA president Neha Bora shared a press-conference platform with the CJP founder and called for halting SIR, and she has advocated Imam's release. No formal demand for his release appears in the published demands of either protest. After Bombay High Court Orders Release Of CJP Members, Goa Police Justifies Detention Citing Past Violations, 'Patronising Umar Khalid' +2

The relevance of this tangent is not guilt by association, nor does it invalidate the protests' underlying grievances. It is to examine whether parallels exist in the political reasoning and strategic remedies advocated by some of the individuals in their orbit. Those parallels must stand on their own arguments, not on the mere appearance of a name or the sharing of a platform.

The comparison is about method, not company. A placard and a shared stage are not a shared programme, and neither protest demanded Imam's release. But set the reasoning side by side and a pattern shows. Each starts from a real grievance, centres it on a locus (a minister, a Commissioner, a majority), and reaches for a dramatic instrument. Each leaves out a metric. Critics also read his thesis as treating the constitutional order as an obstacle for Muslims, which would sit oddly beside a movement that appeals to that same order.

**The argument at full strength:**

- The Constitution abolished separate electorates and refused religion-based reservations. The 1950 Scheduled Castes Order limited SC status to Hindus, with Sikhs and Buddhists added later, which excluded Dalit Muslims despite comparable deprivation.
- First-past-the-post plus joint electorates dilute a dispersed 14% minority into "token" representation.
- Secular parties extract defensive Muslim votes and deliver little.
- Sedition, preventive detention and UAPA continue colonial control, and Muslims are treated as a "suspect population."
- Education and wealth are fragile without collective power, so incremental fixes fail against a measure like CAA-NRC.
- The remedy is decentralisation, proportional representation and structural group power.

**The counters:**

The 1950 Order is the strongest grievance, and it undermines the theory. It is a specific, challengeable rule with a specific fix: extend eligibility or use targeted sub-categories. It passes all three checks and requires a lawsuit and a statute, not a rewritten constitutional order.

FPTP dilution is sound arithmetic and is conceded. The inference from dilution to religion-based power is a non sequitur. His camp frames its demands as decentralisation and proportional representation, which are mechanism-design choices that Germany operates without sectarian politics. But representation is not the median-income variable. SC reservation had no effect on poverty, while ST reservation reduced it.

The vote-bank trap is an equilibrium. Defensive voting persists because the other side can credibly mobilise. A minority organised on religion supplies the majority with a cleaner focal point, which deepens the polarisation it aims to cure.

The colony analogy is a category error. A colony has no franchise and no courts able to overrule the executive. The Supreme Court denied Imam and Khalid regular bail while granting it to five other accused in the same case, and the Delhi High Court held that their roles differ and parity cannot be sought. Differentiation by alleged role is not treatment of a religion as a suspect class, though defenders note that "role" is the contested term, and the delay of a six-year undertrial period is a process failure on any reading. outlookindiaopindia

Fragility cuts both ways. If wealth can be stripped by a hostile majority, so can seats, through delimitation or capture of enforcement. A Muslim-majority Kashmir with its own constitution still saw the Pandit exodus, with militancy and state failure also in play, so embedded group power did not guarantee safety. The stable guarantee is a universal rule of law, which he dismisses as a trap.

Constant hostility cannot explain variance. Gujarat after 2002 is often cited as a rupture followed by long stretches without comparable violence, though sociologists add that it came with deepened segregation. Pakistan, founded on identity rather than competence, is the cautionary case for identity-first design.

The tool failed the gates. The CAA-NRC concern met gate 2, but the argument that an organised blockade could cut the northeast off from the mainland for a month or two threatens the state's functioning to coerce it. His lawyers say he also called for non-violence, and that deserves weight, but the strategy still alienates the cross-community allies a 14% group requires. SubstackSubstack

No metric. "Collective power" has no target, no date, and no test.

What survives: the FPTP arithmetic and the 1950 grievance, both fixable through targeted, testable policy.

## 6. Romanticism and practicality

Romanticism optimises for the sensation of agency, and practicality optimises for the outcome variable. A resignation, a freeze and a veto each deliver catharsis: a visible act, an instant verdict, no waiting. An audit, a statute with a deadline and a skills pipeline deliver nothing on day one and a measurable result by year three. The discriminating question for any demand is which number moves, by how much, and how it would be known.

## The opposing view

Resignation is a genuine accountability norm and can alter successors' incentives, and the post-Pradhan leak count will test that claim.

> Corruption in UPA stopped after resignations. (⸝⸝> ᴗ•⸝⸝)

A single vivid demand also helps a loose crowd coordinate, and CJP did table a structural charter.

> A Vivid demand always has to be something cathartic and illogical. After having a structural charter, there is no need to wait for changes to be implemented before Partyyyy. (⸝⸝> ᴗ•⸝⸝)

Supporters would add that the government moved only under pressure and that the Supreme Court's monitoring is a product of that pressure.

> Like the pressure from mass protests during hardcore lockdown in COVID-19 caused this petition by judges. (⸝⸝> ᴗ•⸝⸝)

On SIR, defenders would argue that court timelines have historically lagged irreversible harms, which is the justification for street pressure at all.

> Slow courts are a real problem. "So freeze the democracy" is a big jump from a small ladder. (⸝⸝> ᴗ•⸝⸝)
